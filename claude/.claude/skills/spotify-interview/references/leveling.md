# Leveling Guide

## Steps Framework

### Associate Engineer
Primarily focused on learning to become a useful contributor. Little to no industry experience. Expected to move beyond this step within first year.

### Engineer I
Fully ramped-up, useful contributor. Can work independently to solve scoped problems involving multiple systems, but relies on team for architectural advice. Expected to eventually move beyond this step.

### Engineer II
Highly functioning contributor who can identify and work on complex problems and build end-to-end solutions from design to implementation. Contributes as domain specialist or generalist. Can lead smaller efforts and drive them to completion. Lots of room for growth; many choose to remain at this step.

### Senior Engineer
Especially impactful and mature expert with a leading role in designing and implementing systems. May be domain expert, effective generalist, mentor, or team leader. Can initiate cross-team communications and coordinate across multiple teams.

## Rubric Scale (1-4)

| Level | Name | Description |
|-------|------|-------------|
| N/A | Not Applicable | Area not addressed in interview |
| 1 | Focused on Learning | New to skill/discipline, needs time to learn |
| 2 | Independent Contributor (guidance needed) | Solves scoped problems, relies on guidance for architecture |
| 3 | Independent Contributor | Works independently on complex problems, 1-2 prompts OK |
| 4 | Impactful Engineer | Expert, could lead system design, explains tradeoffs |

## Example Scoring: Final Interview

### Associate Engineer
- Programming: 2 (needs support but shows aptitude)
- Domain: 2 (good scope with prompts)
- System Design: 1-2 (theoretical understanding)
- Values: 2+

### Engineer I
- Programming: 2-3 (receives prompts but demonstrates ability)
- Domain: 2 (good scope with understanding)
- System Design: 1-2 (theoretical, transitioning to practical)
- Values: 2+

### Engineer II
- Programming: 3 (fewer prompts, competent, confident reasoning)
- Domain: 2-3 (good scope, understands root cause)
- System Design: 2-3 (practical experience demonstrated)
- Values: 3+

### Senior Engineer
- Programming: 3-4 (independent, quality code, clear discussion)
- Domain: 3-4 (demonstrable root cause analysis, efficient solving)
- System Design: 3-4 (wealth of experience, confident design)
- Values: 3+

**Note:** System Design and Values interviews carry heavier significance for Senior-level determination.

## What to Expect by Level

### Associate (~0-1 years)
- Solid fundamentals being learned
- Needs significant guidance on architecture
- Basic coding ability with frequent hints
- Shows enthusiasm and learning aptitude

### Engineer I (~1-3 years)
- Solid coding fundamentals
- Can discuss projects but less depth on architecture decisions
- Needs some hints during coding but makes progress
- Understands basic system concepts

### Engineer II (~3-5 years)
- Speaks confidently about system design tradeoffs
- Independent problem-solving with minimal hints
- Clean, idiomatic code
- Can connect design decisions to real experience

### Senior (~5+ years)
- Drives entire design independently
- Anticipates problems before prompted
- Deep expertise in multiple areas
- Discusses organizational/process implications
- Cross-team thinking and coordination

### Staff (~8+ years)
- Technical vision and strategy
- Cross-org influence and impact
- Mentors and grows other engineers
- Makes systems-level architectural decisions
- Can explain complex tradeoffs to non-technical stakeholders
